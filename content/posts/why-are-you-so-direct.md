Seven years ago I was interviewed by a Frenchman working in the US. He said he had assumed Chinese people communicated in a roundabout way, and asked why I came across as so direct.

I answered right away. I had come out of investment banking, the pace was fast, and there was never time to take the long way around.

He accepted it, and I walked out thinking I had answered him. I had answered the easy part of it.

Here is the rest. In banking, and later in VC due diligence, asking questions was the actual job. **When someone wants your capital or your signature, answering your questions is part of the arrangement**, so the obligation arrives before you do.

Which brings me back to the interviewer. He ran program management. His job was getting information out of people who did not report to him and had no particular reason to help him. That is responsibility without authority. **Being direct doesn't guarantee you an answer, let alone the fastest one.**

And look at the question itself. Instead of asking how I got people to answer me, he wrapped it in a mild observation about culture and left the real question folded inside, there for me to pick up or ignore. The way he asked was itself the answer, which is, now that I think about it, a very French way to ask a Chinese person about indirectness.
