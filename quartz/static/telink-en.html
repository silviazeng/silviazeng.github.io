① Market size & trends

Peripherals and single-mode BLE lead growth; edge AI is upside

Sources: Bluetooth SIG, ABI Research
Bluetooth device shipments are forecast to grow about 8.4% a year in 2025–2030. Dual-mode, mainly in platform devices, is mature (about 2% a year); growth comes mainly from peripherals and single-mode BLE. Edge AI may add further upside and is worth tracking and investing in early.

② Key players · Competitive strategy

Where each company spends reflects how it competes

CompanyCompetitive strategy
Silicon LabsFull multiprotocol stack: own protocol stacks and dev tools, chips alongside pre-certified modules; opex ratio 67% (R&D 45.0%, SG&A 22.2%)
NordicBLE-led revenue + developer on-ramp: nRF Connect SDK and DevZone engage developers, modules left to third parties; opex ratio ~48% (reported by nature of expense)
QorvoExpanding from RF front-end: core business is handset RF front-end; connectivity SoCs and UWB came from the GreenPeak and Decawave acquisitions; merger with Skyworks pending Chinese and Korean regulatory approval
EspressifOpen-source ecosystem + modules: ESP-IDF and the open-source community bring in customers; modules and dev kits are 61.2% of revenue; opex ratio 30% (R&D 23.5%, SG&A and other 6.9%)
TelinkMultiprotocol single-chip SoCs, sold as chips: BLE, Zigbee, Thread and 2.4G proprietary on one chip, with Matter support (application layer, over Thread); opex ratio 42% (R&D 27.2%, SG&A and other 15.1%)
From lowest to highest opex ratio: Espressif, Telink, Nordic, Silicon Labs, the exact reverse of the net margin ranking. Telink spends 27.2% of revenue on R&D, more than Espressif, yet its opex ratio stays below Nordic’s and Silicon Labs’.
Opex ratio = R&D, selling and G&A expenses ÷ revenue (FY2025, incl. stock comp); China A-share companies also include taxes and surcharges. Nordic is gross profit minus EBIT, which includes some production and test costs and partly capitalized R&D, so it is not fully comparable. Sources: annual and quarterly reports, Telink CEO interview

④ Telink’s US & Europe expansion path

A workable growth path

Strengths

Cost-performance (high-volume platform): BLE applications are cost-sensitive, which makes it easier to gain a foothold first.

Moving upmarket

In the US and Europe, the high end values local support and fast response: the focus once Telink has scale and a firm footing.

A practical sequence

① Build scale first in cost-sensitive applications

② Deepen local support for existing key accounts

③ Then move upmarket to capture more value
